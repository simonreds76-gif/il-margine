## NEW SECTION 1: WHERE WE FIND VALUE

### Heading
**Market Selection Philosophy**

### Content

Not every market is worth betting. Most aren't.

Bookmakers dedicate their resources unevenly. Premier League match odds get teams of traders, live monitoring, and sophisticated algorithms. Player props get template pricing and minimal oversight.

**Our focus areas:**

**Player Props**  
Individual player statistics: shots on target, fouls committed, tackles made, cards received. These markets receive fraction of the analytical attention that match odds do. Bookmakers use historical averages with minimal matchup-specific adjustment. When opponent defensive stats, tactical setups, or referee tendencies create outliers, value emerges.

**Tennis Handicaps**  
Game spreads and totals in ATP events, particularly 250s and Challengers. These tournaments get less market attention than Grand Slams. Surface-specific performance differentials are systematically underweighted. A player who's elite on clay but average on grass will still be priced closer to their universal ranking than their surface-specific form suggests.

**Bet Builders** *(Coming Soon)*  
Same-game multiples where correlation is mispriced. When bookmakers price legs independently that are actually correlated, mathematical edges appear. This requires different modeling than singles but follows the same principle: find where the price is wrong.

**What we don't bet:**  
High-profile match odds in major leagues unless clear mispricing exists. We're not competing with bookmaker traders on markets they prioritize. We operate where their models are weakest.

## NEW SECTION 2: THE APPROACH

### Heading
**How Value Betting Actually Works**

### Content

**It's not about predicting winners.**

Most people think betting is about being right. It's not. It's about finding situations where the bookmaker's price doesn't reflect true probability.

**Example:**  
- Bookmaker offers 2.10 odds on a player prop
- Their implied probability: 47.6%
- Our calculated probability: 53%
- Edge: 5.4%

That's a value bet. Even if it loses (which it will 47% of the time), the mathematics are positive over 100+ repetitions.

**The discipline:**  
- We calculate fair odds for selections we analyze
- We only bet when bookmaker price exceeds fair value by meaningful margin
- We don't bet because we "think" something will happen
- We bet because the odds are wrong

**Variance is real:**  
Even with edge, you'll have losing streaks. 10-bet, 15-bet losing runs happen. This is normal variance, not system failure. Edge manifests over hundreds of bets, not dozens.

**Singles only:**  
Accumulators compound bookmaker margin. A five-fold with 5% margin per leg = 22.6% total margin against you. We don't give away 20%+ edge before the match even starts. Every pick stands on its own merit.

## NEW SECTION 3: FORMER COMPILER ADVANTAGE

### Heading
**Why The Other Side Knows Better**

### Content

Most betting services are run by professional bettors who learned by betting. Valuable, but limited perspective.

I learned by *building the prices* bookmakers use.

**What you see from the compiler side:**

**Template pricing dominates.**  
Player props aren't individually crafted for each match. They're generated from historical averages with standard adjustments. Opponent-specific factors, tactical matchups, situational angles—these often aren't in the template.

**Margin allocation varies.**  
Bookmakers apply different margins to different markets. Match odds: 3-5%. Player props: 10-15%. Less efficient markets get wider margins *and* less sophisticated pricing. That's where edge exists.

**Copy-paste pricing.**  
Smaller bookmakers copy larger ones. When the original price is wrong, everyone's price is wrong. You'll see identical mispricing across 4-5 bookmakers simultaneously.

**Risk management priorities.**  
Bookmakers monitor winning accounts on match odds aggressively. Player props? Less so. They know the markets are soft but accept it because the volume is lower. That's your window.

**This isn't insider information.** It's understanding how the machinery works. When you know where the weaknesses are, you know where to look for value.

## NEW SECTION 4: WHAT WE DON'T DO

### Heading
**Discipline Matters**

### Content

**We don't bet on every match.**  
Some days there are 5 selections. Some days there are zero. Value betting requires patience. Forcing picks to maintain a posting schedule is how you destroy edge.

**We don't promise win rates.**  
58% win rate at 1.90 average odds = profitable.  
65% win rate at 1.50 average odds = long-term loss.  

Win rate without odds context is meaningless. We focus on ROI, not arbitrary percentage of bets won.

**We don't hide losses.**  
Losing bets happen. Losing streaks happen. Variance is part of betting. Anyone who only shows winners is either lying or hasn't bet long enough to hit variance.

**We don't guarantee outcomes.**  
Individual bets are coin flips with weighted probabilities. Nothing is certain. Anyone claiming guaranteed winners is either delusional or scamming.

**We don't share the model.**  
Picks are shared. The proprietary modeling isn't. If the exact methodology was public, it would cease to be an edge. This is standard for any professional betting operation.

We target positive ROI across market types. Player props historically higher ROI than tennis (wider margins = more exploitable). Exact figures vary by market and time period. See [Track Record](/track-record) for current data.

### Q: Do you bet on match odds or just props?

Primarily player props and tennis handicaps. Occasionally match odds when clear mispricing exists. We bet wherever edge exists, but focus on less efficient markets where value is more frequent.

---

### Q: Why don't you share your model?

Same reason card counters don't publish their exact systems. If the methodology was public, bookmakers would adjust and the edge would disappear. Picks are shared, the proprietary modeling isn't.

---

### Q: How do you strip bookmaker margin?

We calculate fair odds for each outcome based on our probability assessment, then compare to bookmaker odds which include their margin. The gap between fair odds and offered odds reveals value opportunities. The exact modeling is proprietary.

---

### Q: What if I don't get the same odds you post?

Odds move. By the time we post, sharp money may have moved the line. Try to get close, but don't force bets at significantly worse odds. If you can't get within 5-10% of posted odds, skip that selection.
