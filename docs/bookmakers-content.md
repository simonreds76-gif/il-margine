## SECTION 2: BOOKMAKER TIERS

### Heading
**Understanding The Landscape**

### Tier 1: Sharp Books (Efficient Markets)
**Characteristics:**
- Accept large stakes
- Rarely limit winners
- Odds are razor-sharp
- Minimal edge available

**Examples:**
- Pinnacle
- Betfair Exchange
- Asian bookmakers (Singbet, SBObet)

**Use Case:**
Price benchmarking. If your odds are better than Pinnacle, you probably have edge.

**For Il Margine Tips:**
Not recommended for our player props. Limits are low, odds are poor.

---

### Tier 2: Recreational Books (Soft Markets)
**Characteristics:**
- Mass market focus
- Wide margins on niche markets
- Aggressive limiting of winners
- Excellent for short-term value

**Examples:**
- Bet365
- William Hill
- Ladbrokes
- Coral

**Use Case:**
Primary targets for player props and ATP tennis. These books have soft pricing on markets they don't prioritize.

**For Il Margine Tips:**
**Primary recommendation.** These bookmakers frequently misprice player props by 5-10%.

---

### Tier 3: Price Comparison Sites (Arbitrage Opportunities)
**Characteristics:**
- Aggregate odds from multiple books
- Useful for finding best available price
- Don't take bets directly

**Examples:**
- Oddschecker
- OddsPortal
- BetBrain

**Use Case:**
Always check before placing a bet. Same selection can vary 15-20% between bookmakers.

---

### Tier 4: Betting Exchanges (The Professional's Tool)
**Characteristics:**
- Peer-to-peer betting
- Commission-based model (2-5%)
- Can lay bets (bet against outcomes)
- Harder to get limited

**Examples:**
- Betfair Exchange
- Smarkets
- Matchbook

**Use Case:**
Hedging positions when limited by traditional books. Less value on player props (thin liquidity), but useful for tennis.

**For Il Margine Tips:**
Secondary option. Some tennis tips work well on exchanges. Player props less so.

## SECTION 5: ACCOUNT MANAGEMENT

### Heading
**Maximizing Longevity**

### Strategy 1: The "Mug Punter" Profile
**Concept:**
Make yourself look like a recreational bettor, not a sharp.

**How:**
- Bet on high-profile matches occasionally (even if no value)
- Place some accumulator bets (bookmakers love accas)
- Use cash-out feature occasionally
- Bet during peak times (Saturday 3pm, Champions League nights)
- Avoid only betting on obscure player props

**Effectiveness:**
Can extend account life by 2-3 months.

**Controversy:**
Some argue this involves "deliberate losing bets." Depends on your perspective.

---

### Strategy 2: Variable Stake Sizing
**Concept:**
Don't always max-bet. Vary your stakes to look less systematic.

**How:**
- £200 on one bet
- £50 on the next
- £120 on the third

**Why:**
Bookmakers flag accounts that always bet identical amounts. Looks automated.

---

### Strategy 3: Avoid Pattern Betting
**Concept:**
Don't only bet player props. Don't only bet 2 hours before kick-off.

**How:**
- Mix player props with occasional match odds
- Bet at different times (not always 7pm the night before)

**Why:**
Predictable patterns are easier to flag algorithmically.

---

### Strategy 4: Accept The Inevitable
**Reality:**
No matter what you do, you will get limited.

**Mindset:**
Extract maximum value while you can. Then move to the next bookmaker.

**Long-term:**
You'll cycle through bookmakers. That's fine. That's professional betting.

## SECTION 6: RED FLAGS

### Heading
**Warning Signs You're About to Get Limited**

### Signs:
1. **"Your account is under review"** - They're checking if you're winning
2. **Max stake suddenly drops** - From £200 to £50 overnight
3. **Bets take longer to settle** - Especially player props
4. **You get an email asking about your betting strategy** - They're building a case
5. **Your bets are "referred to traders"** - They're manually checking your selections

### What To Do:
- Cash out your balance
- Move to next bookmaker
- Don't waste time arguing

Bookmakers have the right to limit anyone. Fighting it is pointless.
